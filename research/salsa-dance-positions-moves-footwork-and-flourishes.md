# Salsa Dance: Positions, Moves, Footwork & Flourishes
## Overview
Salsa is one of the world's most popular social dances, built on an 8-count rhythmic structure where dancers step on beats 1–2–3 (pause 4) and 5–6–7 (pause 8). It exists in distinct structural styles — linear and rotational — and at every level from beginner to advanced, dancers layer technique, personality, and musicality onto a core vocabulary of positions, partner moves, solo footwork ("shines"), and decorative embellishments. Understanding all four layers gives a complete picture of the dance.[^1][^2]

***
## Salsa Styles: Structural Foundations
Before cataloguing the moves, it helps to know that the same vocabulary is executed differently depending on the style.[^2]

| Style | Structure | Timing | Character |
|-------|-----------|--------|-----------|
| **LA Style** | Linear (slot) | On1 | Sharp, flashy, acrobatic; Hollywood influence; strong swing roots[^2] |
| **NY Style (Mambo)** | Linear (slot) | On2 | Smooth, elegant, refined; influences from mambo, ballet, jazz, tap[^2] |
| **Cuban / Casino** | Rotational (circular) | On1 (historically On2/On3) | Partners walk around each other; Afro-Cuban folkloric elements; both partners step forward[^2][^3] |
| **Rueda de Casino** | Rotational (group circle) | On1 | Multiple couples; a "caller" shouts moves; partners rotate around the circle[^2] |
| **Colombian / Cali** | Rotational | On1 (sometimes On3) | Ultra-fast syncopated footwork ("repique"); minimal arm patterns; highly acrobatic in shows[^2] |
| **Puerto Rican** | Mostly linear | Any/all timings | Emphasis on clave rhythm; circular arm movements; shoulder shimmies; open shines[^3] |

## Partner Positions
Every salsa pattern begins and ends in a defined position. Knowing these positions allows dancers to transition fluidly between moves.[^4]
### Core Holds
- **Closed Position** — Dancers face each other; the leader's right hand is on the follower's left upper back; the follower's left hand rests on the leader's left shoulder. Used for boleros and more intimate sections.[^5][^4]
- **Half-Closed Position** — A lighter version of closed: leader's right hand on follower's upper back, but with more space between the bodies.[^4]
- **Open Position (Parallel Hold)** — Dancers face each other holding both pairs of hands (right-to-left, left-to-right). The most common social dance position.[^4]
- **Half-Open Right-to-Left** — One-hand hold: leader's right hand holds follower's left hand.[^4]
- **Half-Open Left-to-Right** — One-hand hold: leader's left hand holds follower's right hand.[^4]
### Crossed / Advanced Holds
- **Handshake Hold** — Dancers face each other in a right-to-right handhold, often used as a transition grip.[^4]
- **Reverse Handshake** — Left-to-left handhold; mirror of the handshake.[^4]
- **Crosshold** — Both pairs of hands held with arms crossed: right-to-right and left-to-left, with leader's right over left. Commonly reached from a double-handed turn.[^4]
- **Reverse Crosshold** — Same as crosshold but leader's left hand is over right.[^4]
- **Crossed Parallel Hold** — Left-to-right and right-to-left, often reached from a double turn in the opposite spin direction.[^4]
### Orientation Variants
Dancers can face the same direction, stand side-by-side, or face opposite directions. Using shorthand: **FEO** (Facing Each Other), **FSD** (Facing Same Direction), **SBS** (Side By Side), **LIF** (Leader In Front), **LIB** (Leader In Back). These orientations combine with the handholds above to produce dozens of named and unnamed positions.[^4]

## Foundational Footwork & Basic Steps
The basic step is a 6-step pattern across 8 beats, with pauses on beats 4 and 8.[^1]
### The Five Core Basics
1. **Basic on the Spot** — Weight shifts in place; the building block for all timing and rhythm.[^6]
2. **Side Basic** — Steps out side-to-side instead of forward-and-back.[^1][^6]
3. **Back Basic** — Steps backward on a diagonal; sometimes called the "atrás step".[^7]
4. **Crossing Back Basic (Cumbia Basic / "Quintas")** — A cross-behind step used widely in Colombian style.[^6]
5. **Front-to-Back Basic** — The classic linear version; leader steps forward-pause-back, follower mirrors.[^8]

## Beginner Partner Moves
These are the "lego blocks" from which all other salsa vocabulary is built.[^9]
### Level 1
| Move | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Right Turn** | A 360° single turn to the follower's right; the first spin learned[^9][^6] |
| **Left Turn** | A 360° single turn to the follower's left; sets up cross-body inside turns[^6] |
| **Cross Body Lead (CBL)** | The foundational linear move: leader steps to the side and opens the "slot" for the follower to walk across and swap sides[^1] |
| **Check / Hesitation / Block** | A deliberate stop-and-weight-hold that creates musical accents and pauses[^9] |
| **New York Walk** | A CBL variation where the follower pivots right instead of left to finish the move[^6] |
| **Change of Place / Enchufa** | The simplest leader-follower position swap; a cornerstone of Cuban and Colombian salsa[^6] |
### Level 2
- **Cross Body Lead Inside Turn** — CBL with the follower spinning left (inside) during the crossing.[^9]
- **Cross Body Lead Outside Turn** — CBL with the follower spinning right (outside).[^9]
- **CBL Variations** — Modified arm placements, handhold changes, or timing tweaks on the cross body lead.[^9]
- **Change of Place Progressions** — Using different handholds and directions on the Enchufa.[^6][^9]

***
## Intermediate Partner Moves
Intermediate moves introduce variations, partner wraps, and more complex footwork.[^9]

| Move | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Cross Body Open** | CBL that opens into a wide traveling step rather than a tight pivot[^9] |
| **Pull Through** | Leader pulls the follower through in front of them rather than opening a side slot[^9] |
| **Tow Truck / Basket** | A wrap where the follower is temporarily "captured" in an arm cradle[^9] |
| **Arm Spiral** | A turning arm motion that creates a corkscrew spiral effect on the follower's arms[^9] |
| **Copa** | A classic move where the leader frames the follower's face with their hand as she turns[^9] |
| **Cape** | Leader uses an arm like a matador's cape to direct the follower's spin direction[^9] |
| **Diva Walks** | Stylized walks where the follower struts forward or in an arc while the leader steps back[^9] |
| **Walk Around** | Partners rotate around a shared center point while maintaining a handhold[^9] |
| **Partner Swing Step** | A momentum-based rock that swings both partners simultaneously[^9] |
| **Double Turns** | Follower (and sometimes leader) completes two full rotations in one move[^9] |
| **Dile Que No** | Cuban/Casino fundamental: a side-step sequence that resets partners to open position (literally "tell her no")[^10] |
| **Enchufla** | Cuban/Rueda move: a half-turn pass-by where both partners turn into and past each other[^11][^10] |
| **Setenta** | A Cuban figure combining multiple arm wraps and turns into a flowing sequence[^10] |

## Advanced Partner Moves
Advanced salsa focuses on spinning quantity, non-conventional turn entries, and acrobatic flourishes.[^9]
### Multi-Turn Spins
- **Multiple turns 3+** — Three or more consecutive spins on the spot, requiring a strong axis and precise spotting technique.[^9]
- **Inside 2.5 Turns** — An inside (left) turn with a half-turn added so the follower finishes facing a different direction.[^9]
- **Outside 2.5 Turns** — Same concept applied to the outside (right) turn.[^9]
- **Late Inside / Outside Turn** — Turns initiated on a delayed beat, creating musical contrast.[^9]
- **Overturns** — Turns that intentionally rotate past 360°, used for musical accent or trick entries.[^9]
### Spin Technique
Spotting is critical: the dancer fixes their gaze on a fixed point and snaps their head around quickly with each rotation to maintain orientation and prevent dizziness. Multiple spins require a compressed arm position, a single-point pivot on the ball of the foot, and an engaged core for stability.[^12][^13]
### Dips, Drops & Acrobatics
These moves are characteristic of performance-level LA style and Colombian show salsa.[^14]

| Move | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Back Dip** | Follower arches backward supported at the waist; leader maintains control throughout[^15] |
| **Death Drop** | An advanced dip where the follower "drops" to the floor: left leg bent, right leg straight, weight pressing forward — initiated from a CBL with a break-back[^16] |
| **Yo-Yo** | A series of arm leads that spin the follower in and out like a yo-yo; often used before a death drop combination[^17] |
| **Lifts** | Full aerial lifts where the follower leaves the floor; strictly performance territory requiring significant strength and training[^18] |

## Shines: Solo Footwork
Shines are solo footwork sequences performed when partners break apart. They are fundamental to NY and LA style, and a core feature of Puerto Rican salsa. They develop timing, musicality, foot speed, weight transfer, and personal expression.[^2][^19][^20][^3]
### Core Shine Steps
| Shine | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Suzie Q (Susie Q)** | The most iconic shine: a rolling lateral hip-and-foot slide, alternating sides across counts 1–3 and 5–7[^21][^22] |
| **Mambo Step** | Quick-quick-slow tapping pattern emphasizing the mambo's characteristic rhythm[^22] |
| **Zeta** | A crossover footwork pattern creating a Z-shaped path[^22] |
| **Tres Tres** | A three-step repetition pattern on each side[^22] |
| **Kick Ball Change** | A sharp kick followed by a weight-transferring ball-change step[^22] |
| **Crossover** | A traveling shine where feet cross over each other as the dancer moves laterally[^22] |
| **Braid** | Feet alternate in a weaving pattern, resembling the structure of a braid[^22] |
| **Grapevine** | A side-traveling step where the back foot crosses behind then the front foot steps out[^22] |
| **Son Basic** | The foundational Afro-Cuban son rhythm step, emphasizing the clave[^22] |
| **Flare Step** | A stepping-and-tapping pattern where the foot taps out on even counts (2, 4, 6, 8) and steps on the core beats — the dancer lowers their level slightly for smooth execution[^23] |
### Suzie Q Variations
The Suzie Q alone generates a rich sub-vocabulary:[^21]
- **Suzie Back Step** — Adds a backward step for a different spatial intention
- **Suzie Walks** — Opens with two chord-beat walks before the Suzie
- **Suzie Plus** — Full 8-count Suzie in one direction, then reversal for another 8
- **Suzie Point** — Adds a jazz point (flared foot) on the 4th beat of each side
- **Jazz Point with Suzie Q** — Jazz points bookend a Suzie Q in the middle
- **Suzie with Mambo Taps** — Merges Suzie Q with mambo-style tapping on beats 5–7
- **In-and-Outs with Suzie Q** — Dynamic in-and-out leg motion on beats 1–3 before the Suzie Q on 5–7

## Body Movement & Technique
Good salsa technique is not just footwork — it involves the entire kinetic chain from the ground up.
### Hip Motion
Hip movement in salsa comes from proper weight transfer, not from forcing the hips. As the dancer pushes horizontally into the floor and shifts weight, the hip of the weighted leg naturally settles. Three core hip movements are trained independently: **side-to-side**, **forward-and-back**, and **circular hip circles**. Cuban style places extra emphasis on Afro-Cuban hip and body isolations.[^24][^25][^14]
### Upper Body & Ribcage Isolation
All arm movement originates from ribcage motion. When the ribcage moves, the shoulder blades follow, and relaxed arms swing naturally. Key isolation drills train the ribcage to move side-to-side and circularly independently of the lower body. Chest and shoulder isolations — pushing the shoulder back and releasing — are executed with a compressed, stabilized core so the lower body stays grounded.[^26][^27]
### Footwork Mechanics
Unlike natural walking (which is upper-body-led), salsa involves an active horizontal push off the floor. Knees stay relaxed to avoid tension and bouncing. Weight is clearly committed to one foot at a time to keep the free foot available for motion. This grounded quality is what gives experienced dancers their characteristic smooth, non-bouncy quality.[^24]

## Flourishes, Embellishments & Adornments
Flourishes are decorative additions layered on top of the core choreography. They add personality, musicality, and visual flair without disrupting the move structure.
### Arm Styling
- **Arm Circles** — Flowing circular arm motions timed to hip and ribcage movement, creating the characteristic "liquid" look.[^26]
- **Wrist Rolls** — Rotating the wrist while the arm extends, adding softness or sharpness depending on intent.[^26]
- **Finger Framing** — Shaping the hands and fingers elegantly during free-arm moments; can be soft and flowing or sharp and staccato.[^26]
- **Arms Overhead** — Reaching one or both arms up during turns or pauses for theatrical effect.
- **Leader Arm Variations** — The same turn executed with four different arm positions creates four visually distinct moves.[^28]
### Head Movements
- **Head Flick** — A sharp snap of the head, often used on accents or at the start of a cross body lead.[^29]
- **Head Roll** — A slow, smooth circular head rotation used during pauses or slow musical passages.[^29]
- **Boogaloo / Pachanga Head** — A rhythmic, bounce-synced head movement tied to the boogaloo side step.[^29]
### Follower Foot Embellishments
- **Toe Points** — Pointing the toe during any step or pause for elegant line extension.
- **Leg Flicks / Kicks** — Quick, controlled kicks or flicks of the lower leg during the pause beats (4 and 8), timed to musical accents.
- **Heel Digs** — Placing the heel down sharply on an accent beat.
- **Syncopated Steps** — Adding extra footwork steps between the main beats to respond to percussion or piano syncopations.[^30]
### Inside Turn Variations
The cross-body inside turn alone has at least seven known variations:[^31]
- Shoulder turn variation
- Hip turn variation
- Free spin variation
- Wrap with a check
- Wrap with complete turn
- Butterfly with a check
- Butterfly with complete turn
- Barrel roll
- Sliding doors (applicable to shoulder, hip, and free spin versions)

## Cuban / Rueda de Casino Exclusive Vocabulary
Rueda de Casino (group circle salsa) uses a dedicated called-move vocabulary:[^10]

| Move | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Dame** | "Give me" — the caller commands everyone to pass their follower to the next leader[^10] |
| **Dame Dos** | Pass two followers ahead[^10] |
| **Adios** | A move where the follower spins out and away from the leader[^10] |
| **Enchufla Doble** | Double enchufla — two pass-by turns in sequence[^10] |
| **Sombrero** | "Hat" — a move where the leader's hand passes over the follower's head[^10] |
| **Kentucky** | A complex rotating figure named after the Kentucky Derby[^10] |
| **Pelota** | "Ball" — a figure involving circular walking around each other[^10] |
| **Setenta** | A 70-count sequence combining wraps, turns, and frame changes[^10] |
| **Candado** | "Padlock" — a closing lock position used to end sequences[^10] |
| **Balsero** | A rocking "rafter" motion named after Cuban rafters[^10] |
| **Pa'ti Pa'mi** | "For you, for me" — an alternating outward-and-inward spin figure[^10] |

## Progressive Learning Path
Building a complete salsa vocabulary requires a structured approach:

1. **Foundations** — Master all five basics, right and left turns, and the cross body lead
2. **Partner mechanics** — Learn the CBL variations, change of place, hesitation, and New York walk
3. **Shines introduction** — Add Suzie Q, grapevine, and kick ball change as solo footwork
4. **Intermediate partner work** — Copa, cape, double turns, arm spirals, tow truck
5. **Body movement** — Layer hip isolations, ribcage movement, and arm styling onto existing steps
6. **Advanced turns** — Multiple spins, 2.5 turns, late turns, overturns
7. **Embellishments** — Head movements, foot flicks, wrist rolls, syncopated steps
8. **Performance moves** — Dips, drops (with proper training and a trusted partner), Rueda vocabulary

The magic is not in the number of moves but in the creative recombination of them, responsiveness to the music, and connection with a partner.[^9]
