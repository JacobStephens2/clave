# Clave — A Partner Dance Game (Salsa): Rules

Draft v0.1 (2026-07-12). Compiled from the design notes in this repo, giving highest
privilege to the July 2026 journal transcriptions (`handwritten-notes/transcriptions/journal_20260711_*`),
which contain the most recent rules drafts (notebook pp. 84–97, written 5/26–6/15/26).
Earlier material (April 2026 brainstorm, synthesis, living documents) fills gaps where it
doesn't conflict.

Each rule is tagged:

- **[settled]** — stated as a rule in the latest notes.
- **[provisional]** — from earlier notes or inferred to make the rules playable; confirm in playtest.
- Open items are gathered in [Open Questions](#12-open-questions).

## 1. Introduction

Clave is a Partner Dance Game in which two players cooperatively play as dance partners
in a salsa song. Your goal is to connect with your partner and the music in style.
**[settled]** (p. 84)

One player is the **Lead** and the other is the **Follow**. Together you play cards —
positions, moves, and flourishes — to dance through the sections of a real salsa song,
earning style points and maintaining your connection. You win or lose together.

## 2. Components

**Dance cards** **[settled]** (p. 84)

- Beginner dance card sets (one starter set per player)
- Intermediate dance card sets (lessons market)
- Advanced dance card sets (lessons market)

**Song cards** **[settled]** (p. 84) — one deck per song, split by section:

- Cuerpo cards (with marked phrase starts / phrase ends)
- Montuno cards (with marked phrase starts / phrase ends)

**Trackers** **[provisional]**

- Connection track (shared)
- Style point total (shared)

## 3. Card Types & Anatomy

Three dance card types **[settled]** (Apr 18 notes, carried through all later drafts):

1. **Position** — the state the dancers are in: open, closed, half-open, half-closed,
   shines, promenade, cuddle.
2. **Move** — turns, cross body leads, walks, etc.
3. **Flourish** — an embellishment played on top of a move (e.g. arm overhead, hand
   twist), which may carry its own requirement such as a free hand.

Card anatomy **[settled]** (p. 85):

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Type | move |
| Name | Cross Body Lead |
| Style points | 2 |
| Position tags | open, closed |

**Position tags** are the matching mechanism: a move card lists the position(s) it can
be played from, like a suit. A move playable from several positions carries several
tags. **[settled]** (pp. 70, 80)

## 4. Setup

1. **Starting decks.** Each player assembles a starting deck of 8 cards — these
   represent moves learned in beginner salsa lessons **[settled]** (p. 95, 6/15/26):
   - 4 × Open Position
   - 1 × Follow Right Turn
   - 1 × Follow Left Turn
   - 1 × Lead Right Turn
   - 1 × Lead Left Turn
2. **Draw hands.** Each player shuffles their deck and draws four cards as their
   starting hand. **[settled]** (p. 95)
3. **Song deck.** Build the song deck for the chosen song: cuerpo cards, then montuno
   cards, in section order. **[provisional]** (pp. 48, 78; songs may be prebuilt, or each
   section shuffled within itself for variety)
4. **Roles & turn order.** Decide who is Lead and who is Follow. The Lead is the start
   player; turn order is fixed (Lead → Follow → Lead → …). **[settled]** (pp. 49, 78)
5. **Connection.** Set the connection tracker to its starting value (to be set in
   playtesting). **[provisional]**
6. **Lessons market** (if using the Lessons module, §9). Reveal 2–3 intermediate lesson
   cards face up. **[provisional]** (p. 81)

## 5. Turn Structure

Turns alternate in fixed order, Lead first. On your turn, in sequence **[settled]**
(pp. 78, 95):

1. **Play cards.** Play anywhere from zero cards up to **two position cards and one
   move card** (maximum 3 cards), subject to the play requirements in §6.
2. **Discard.** You may discard any number of cards from your hand.
3. **Draw.** Draw back up to a hand of four.

When your personal deck runs out, shuffle your discard pile to form a new deck and keep
drawing. **[settled]** (p. 81)

> Note: an earlier draft (p. 81, 5/25/26) capped play at one card per turn; the 6/15/26
> draft (p. 95) supersedes it with the 0–3 card structure above.

## 6. Card Play Requirements (Matching)

Matching is the key mechanism of Clave. **[settled]** (pp. 51, 97)

- **A move card must match the last played position card** — the move's position tags
  must include the position currently on the board. **[settled]** (p. 97)
- **The first play of the game and of each phrase must include both a position card and
  a move card.** This establishes the position the dancers start in. **[settled]** (p. 95)
- If there is no card on the board, any position may be led. **[settled]** (p. 78)
- A position card played after a move sets the new current position (e.g. open position
  → cross body lead → closed position). Playing a second position card on your turn is
  how the couple transitions. **[settled]** (pp. 85, 95)
- **Flourishes** are played on top of a move card and must satisfy that flourish's own
  requirement (e.g. a free hand in the current position). **[provisional]** (Apr 18 notes)

### Recovery **[provisional]** (pp. 50–51, 55, 56, 78)

If you cannot legally play (nothing in hand matches the board) and you must — or you
simply choose not to play — the dance stumbles:

- Clear the dance state (the board is empty again; the next play must re-establish
  position + move, as at a phrase start).
- Lose 1 connection.

Phrases danced **without** any recovery earn a style bonus (§8).

## 7. The Song: Phrases & Eight-Counts

The song deck is the clock and the scenario. **[settled]** (pp. 48, 78)

- The game lasts a fixed number of rounds: **two phrases — the cuerpo and the montuno**
  — per the song deck. **[settled]** (p. 78)
- Each phrase is a row of **eight 8-counts**. Each 8-count holds one turn's worth of
  played cards (position / move / flourish slots). **[settled]** (pp. 56–57)
- **Progressive reveal:** at the start of each turn, reveal the next song card — the
  music of that 8-count. Play your cards on it, then the next turn reveals the next
  8-count, through the 8th; then the phrase is scored. **[settled]** (p. 69, "more like
  dance w/ progressive reveal")
- Song cards can carry bonuses for matching your play to the music of that 8-count.
  **[provisional]** (p. 79)
- When the song deck runs out, the dance is over. **[settled]** (p. 71)

## 8. Scoring, Winning & Losing

Clave is fully cooperative: both players share one score. **[settled]** (pp. 49, 78)

**Style points** come from the cards you play — each move and flourish card is worth
its printed style points. Harder and more advanced cards are worth more. **[settled]**
(pp. 84–85; Apr 18 notes)

**Scheduled scoring:** score at the end of each phrase (end of cuerpo, end of montuno).
**[settled]** (pp. 56–57, 78)

Phrase-end bonuses **[provisional]** (pp. 50, 53, 56–57, 80):

- **Chains** — longer unbroken position → move → flourish chains score extra.
- **Variety sets** — a phrase whose moves hit multiple positions scores extra.
- **Clean phrase** — no recovery played during the phrase.
- **Ending pose** — bonus if you end the phrase in a pose.
- **Both partners flourish** — bonus when Lead and Follow have each flourished in the
  phrase.

**Losing:** you lose if connection reaches 0. You lose connection when you fail to
follow — i.e., when you must recover instead of matching. **[settled]** (p. 78)

**Winning:** finish the song with connection above 0, then compare your combined style
points against the song's rating board (score thresholds per song). **[provisional]**
(p. 48; exact thresholds and whether connection also feeds the final score are open —
see §12)

## 9. Lessons Module (Deck Building) — optional in v1

The 6/15/26 rules draft runs on starter decks alone, and the v1 scope pass (p. 79)
considers leaving deck building out entirely, so this module is optional for the first
version. When used **[provisional]** (pp. 62–63, 66, 79–81):

- **Income:** each player gains $1 (an hour of practice time) per turn.
- **Group lessons** cost $1; **private lessons** cost $2. A private lesson slot is only
  available to one player per round but offers more learning options.
- **Beginner lessons** are always available (basic, flexible cards). **Intermediate
  lessons** are a rotating market of 2–3 face-up cards; refill the market on purchase.
  Advanced lessons unlock later in the song (e.g. from the montuno on).
- Purchased cards enter your **discard pile** (you'll draw them as your deck recycles —
  practice at home before it shows up on the social floor).
- **Learning from your partner:** once per phrase, a player may draft a copy of a card
  their partner played, representing learning from the dance itself.

## 10. Communication Rules

Your hand is private. You may not tell your partner what is in your hand or discuss
specific cards to set up plays — but you may reveal **one card** from your hand to your
partner as a signal of what you can do. General talk about intentions ("let's stay
open," "big finish?") is allowed. **[settled]** (pp. 63, 79)

## 11. Later Modules (out of scope for v1)

Ideas the notes explicitly defer — parked here so they don't creep into v1:

- **Resolution ladder** (Perfect → Clean → Awkward → Recovered → Break) from the
  concept brief; the v1 notes choose deterministic resolution ("the game can function
  with no uncertain outcomes," p. 78).
- **Individual goal cards / contracts** — semi-coop personal goals that boost the co-op
  score if fulfilled (pp. 48, 66).
- **Worker placement between dances** — practice / teach / assistant-teach actions
  (p. 67).
- **Events** — tight floor, beer spills, dance circle (p. 51).
- **Floorcraft / movement on a dance floor map** — explicitly a non-goal (concept
  brief; p. 80: "no movement tracked beyond position and move cards in first version").
- **Card upgrades** — attaching a flourish permanently to a move, Balatro-style (p. 66).
- **Variable player powers** — named dancers with special strengths (p. 51).
- **Competitive 4-player mode** — two partnerships compare scores (Apr 18 synthesis).
- **Shines / time track** — breaking apart into solo footwork, possibly via interrupts
  (p. 49).

## 12. Open Questions

1. **Starting connection value** — how much rope before the couple loses? (§4.5)
2. **Final score formula** — style alone vs. style + connection, or Tigris-style
   "your score is the lower of the two" to force balance (p. 55).
3. **Song card effects** — what exactly a revealed 8-count asks for and grants (p. 79
   suggests per-8 bonuses for matching the music).
4. **Recovery details** — exact cost and whether a dedicated recovery/reset card exists
   (pp. 51, 56) versus the board-clear rule in §6.
5. **Deck size vs. phrase length** — 8×8-counts may demand more cards than an 8-card
   deck comfortably cycles; notes flag needing >24 cards if cards aren't gathered up
   until phrase end (p. 57).
6. **Move-on-move chaining** — can a move match a prior move (CBL → New York Walk), or
   only positions? (p. 52)
7. **Flourish state tracking** — how "free hand" requirements are checked (Apr 18
   synthesis).
8. **Cierre** — the notes' song structure includes a closing section after the montuno
   (p. 48; `0-dance.md`); is it a scored third mini-phrase or just a finale bonus?
9. **Lessons in v1** — include the Lessons module (§9) in the first playtest or add it
   in the second?
