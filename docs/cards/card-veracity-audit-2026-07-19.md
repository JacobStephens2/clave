# Card Veracity and Expansion Audit

**Date:** 2026-07-19
**Scope:** v1 Positions, Moves, Flourishes, and the eight-card starter deck

## Result

The card vocabulary is broadly recognizable salsa, but its original position tags
treated a figure's handhold too rigidly in some places and incorrectly in two others.
The most important gameplay defect was real: the starter deck contained Closed
Position but no Move that matched Closed, so that Position could not help open a
phrase and could strand the shared dance before lessons entered circulation.

The starter correction keeps 2 Open Positions, 1 Closed Position, the four basic
turns, and a foundational Cross Body Lead. A follow-up content ruling splits that
figure into **Cross Body Lead — Open** and **Cross Body Lead — Closed** so its entry
handhold is visible on the card instead of represented by one universal Move. The
starter carries the Closed version; together with Follow Right Turn it gives Closed
two genuine partners in the deck. The Open version remains an always-available
Beginner lesson.

This split adds one production card while preserving the starter, lesson-supply, and
full-deck copy totals. The remaining expansion candidates are recorded below for a
later balance-and-art pass.

## Interpretation used by the game

A position tag answers: **“Can this figure plausibly begin from this state and carry
out its ordinary re-grip or release inside one 8-count?”** It does not require the
couple to preserve the initial hold for all eight beats.

The four game states are useful, real abstractions:

- **Open:** facing, two-hand hold.
- **Half-Open:** facing, one-hand hold.
- **Closed:** full partner frame.
- **Half-Closed:** one-sided frame.

The [Salsa Handbook position reference](https://salsahandbook.github.io/output/salsa-positions.html)
defines Open as a two-hand facing hold, Half-Open as its one-hand variation, and
Half-Closed as a facing frame using the Lead's right hand on the Follow's back. It
also describes an eight-beat pattern as a transition from a start position to an end
position. That maps cleanly to Clave's Position → Move → optional Position grammar.

Half states do not inherit all matches automatically. A turn that needs one joined
hand can list Half-Open, while a figure that really needs a two-hand pathway can stay
Open-only. Explicit tags keep that distinction in content rather than engine code.

## Evidence base

- The [Imperial Society of Teachers of Dancing 2024 Alternative Rhythms syllabus](https://www.istd.org/documents/alternative-rhythms-syllabus-outline-september-2024/alternative-rhythms-syllabus-september-2024.pdf)
  lists basic right and left turns, Cross Body Lead in both Closed and Open, inside
  and outside Cross Body figures, Walkthrough, Double Turn, and the Cuban figures
  Enchufla, Dile Que No, and Sombrero.
- [Salsa University's beginner curriculum](https://salsa-university.teachable.com/p/introduction-to-salsa-dancing)
  explicitly teaches a Follow right turn from Closed, a Follow left turn from Open,
  and Closed-to-Open right-turn material.
- The [New York Walk curriculum description](https://www.udemy.com/course/learn-how-2-dance-salsa-intermediate/)
  describes that figure as Cross Body Lead footwork in Open Position and later works
  it through multiple handholds.
- The [Enchufla breakdown](https://salsaselfie.com/2024/10/31/cuban-salsa-enchufla-has-four-main-options-after-step-7/)
  explicitly starts the basic figure in Open Position.
- The [Sombrero breakdown](https://only-dance.com/en/sombrero-figure-cuban-salsa/)
  explicitly starts it in Open Position and describes collecting the second hand
  during the figure.
- The [Cambridge “12 Basic Moves of Cuban Salsa” reference](https://www.cambridgesalsa.co.uk/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/The_12_basic_moves_of_Cuban_salsa.pdf)
  treats Enchufla and Dile Que No as foundational units and identifies Sombrero with
  the Vacila family.
- The [USA Dance syllabus guidebook](https://www.usadancela.org/uploads/1/3/5/7/135765103/2016a_syllabus_guidebook-rul.pdf)
  recognizes open and closed basics, underarm turns, Cross Body Lead, and later double
  turns; it also excludes dips at bronze level, supporting Dip's advanced treatment.

Salsa naming and teaching order vary by style and school. Clave's B/I/A tiers are
therefore game progression and reward bands, not claims of universal syllabus rank.

### Flourishes and recoveries

- Both Arm Overhead cards remain “any”: they are styling abstractions rather than a
  claim that the same arm path is used in every hold.
- Hand Twist remains Open/Half-Open, where hand connection is available and visible.
- Dip remains Advanced and Closed-only. That is the narrow, safer rules expression;
  it should not imply that unsupported social-floor dips are routine.
- Return to Basic and Reconnect are game recovery concepts, not named syllabus
  figures, so position veracity does not apply to them.

## Coverage after correction

| Position    | Compatible Moves in the full card set | Compatible Moves in the starter |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------: | ------------------------------: |
| Open        |                                    12 |                               4 |
| Closed      |                                     5 |                               2 |
| Half-Open   |                                    12 |                               4 |
| Half-Closed |                                     4 |                               1 |

The asymmetry is desirable. Open and Half-Open are flexible turn-pattern states;
Closed and especially Half-Closed are more constrained, but no state is a dead end.
The two foundational Cross Body Lead cards divide that beginner bridge by entry
family, while higher-value cards still ask the players to manage position deliberately.

Enumerating the 70 possible four-card opening hands, 64 (91.4%) contain at least one
matching Position/Move pair—the same opening reliability as before the swap. Of the
35 hands that contain Closed Position, 25 (71.4%) now also contain a Move that can be
danced specifically from Closed; previously that number was zero.

## Recommended expansion queue

These are defensible additions, but they should enter only after the revised starter
is physically/digitally playtested. Adding them immediately would change market odds,
deck identity, balance fixtures, and the complete card-art set without solving a
remaining rules hole.

| Priority | Candidate       | Suggested tier / entries  | Why                                                                                            |
| -------- | --------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | Dile Que No     | B · Closed, Half-Closed   | Foundational Cuban counterpart to Enchufla and a second expressive route from a framed hold.   |
| 2        | Open Break      | B · Open, Half-Open       | A fundamental preparation that gives the Open family more non-turning rhythm.                  |
| 3        | Copa / In & Out | I · Open, Half-Open       | Explicitly present in the ISTD syllabus and adds a check/redirection pattern.                  |
| 4        | Guapea          | B · Open, Half-Open       | The core Casino open basic; useful if the game later distinguishes style families.             |
| 5        | 360 or Wrap     | A · Open, Closed variants | Better candidates for genuinely advanced rewards than simply promoting basic Cuban vocabulary. |

Before adding any of these, decide whether Clave wants to identify style families
(linear On1/On2 versus Casino) on cards. The current compact deck intentionally mixes
shared social-salsa vocabulary; expansion will make that design choice more visible.
